1Data and the rules set first

Storyline: before counting anything, each table's grain was checked. Two would have misled a naive count: customer ids are new on every order, and some orders carry several reviews.

The data is the public Olist Brazilian e-commerce dataset: orders placed on a marketplace of small independent sellers between September 2016 and October 2018. Sellers here play the role restaurants play on a platform like Owner.com: small merchants trying to grow online sales. Every number in this report comes from tested dbt models and is reproduced by a separate pandas script.

    6Limits and method

    • Marketplace data, not software usage data. It shows orders, deliveries, reviews and seller activity. It does not show feature adoption or website conversion, and no events were invented to fill that gap.
    • Associations, not causes. Seller comparisons rank where to look. Sellers who start small or get bad reviews may differ in product, price or effort.
    • Sizing shares are assumptions. The share of sellers moved and the share of the gap credited are inputs, shown so they can be changed or replaced by a test result.
    • Sales, not revenue. Values are item prices in Brazilian reais (R$). Olist's commission is not in the data.
    • Order-level measures. Review score and lateness belong to the whole order; on multi-seller orders each seller carries them.

    Method. dbt on DuckDB: 6 staging views, 2 intermediate and 4 mart tables, 25 data tests including exact reconciliation of item value across every mart. One script per question. checks/independent_check.py rebuilds 14 headline numbers from the raw CSVs in pandas, sharing no code with the models; all 14 match.
